Writing at this level must be clear, precise, and accessible. Avoid jargon where a plain word works. Each paragraph should state a clear point and support it effectively.

When editing:
- Every sentence should earn its place. Remove filler, redundancy, and throat-clearing.
- The introduction should state the research question and contribution within the first few paragraphs.
- Hypothesis development should follow a clear logical chain: theory, prediction, rationale.
- Results sections should report findings plainly without overclaiming.
- The conclusion should not simply repeat the introduction.

Typical weaknesses to watch for in journal submissions: vague motivation statements, overuse of passive voice, hedging that undermines the argument, and findings described as "interesting" or "important" without explaining why.
