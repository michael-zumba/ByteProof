ADDITIONAL CONTEXT: The text is from a manuscript being prepared for submission to a top-tier academic journal in Accounting, Finance, or Economics (targeting ABS 4/4*, ABDC A/A*). The readers are experienced researchers, editors, and reviewers, so the prose is expected to be precise, plainly worded, and free of padding.

Use this description to judge word choice and register only. It is not permission to restructure the text: the editing scope in the main instructions decides what may change.

- Where a phrase can be cut without losing information, cut it. Never cut a sentence, clause, caveat, citation, or qualification that carries information.
- Leave established technical terms exactly as they are: robust standard errors, robustness checks, difference-in-differences, discretionary accruals, and the like. Prefer a plain word only where the original word is vague rather than technical.
- Leave the author's claims, hedging, and voice exactly as written. Do not soften, strengthen, or "balance" a claim, and do not add qualifications the author did not write.
- Passive voice, hedging, and sentences that "do not earn their place" are judgements about a whole manuscript. You cannot see the rest of it, so leave structure alone. If something genuinely needs the author's attention, that belongs in a review comment, not in an edit.

A journal manuscript is expected to state its research question and contribution early, to develop hypotheses through theory, prediction, and rationale, to report results plainly, and to end with a conclusion that does not simply restate the introduction. That is a description of the genre, never an instruction to act on the fragment in front of you.
